Atomic Number, Mass Number & Isotopes

  • Atomic number (Z): Number of protons
  • Mass number (A): Protons + neutrons
  • Isotopes: Atoms of same element with different numbers of neutrons
  • Relative atomic mass (Ar): Average mass of isotopes weighted by abundance
Ar = (mass1 x %1 + mass2 x %2) / 100

Electronic Configuration

Rules: Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion (max 2 e- per orbital), Hund's rule (fill singly first)

Shell (n)SubshellsMax e-
11s2
22s, 2p8
33s, 3p, 3d18
44s, 4p, 4d, 4f32

Order of filling: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p...

Cr: [Ar] 3d^5 4s^1 (exception, half-filled stability)

Cu: [Ar] 3d^10 4s^1 (exception, full d stability)

Ionisation Energy

Energy required to remove 1 mole of electrons from 1 mole of gaseous atoms.

X(g) -> X+(g) + e-

Trends:

  • Increases across a period (increasing nuclear charge, same shielding)
  • Decreases down a group (more shielding, greater distance)
  • Drops between groups 2 and 3 (p electron, more shielded)
  • Drops between groups 5 and 6 (pairing in p orbital, repulsion)

Ionic Bonding

  • Transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal
  • Form giant ionic lattice
  • Lattice energy: Energy released when gaseous ions form 1 mole of solid lattice
  • Higher charge and smaller ions = higher lattice energy
Lattice energy proportional to (q+ x q-) / (r+ + r-)

Covalent Bonding

  • Sharing of electron pairs
  • Bond length: Distance between nuclei. Shorter = stronger.
  • Bond energy: Energy required to break bond. Higher = stronger.
  • Coordinate (dative): Both electrons from one atom (e.g., NH4+, Al2Cl6)

Lone pairs repel more than bonding pairs, reducing bond angles.

Polarity

  • Polar bond: Between atoms with different electronegativities
  • Polar molecule: Has a dipole moment (asymmetrical shape or polar bonds don't cancel)
  • Examples: H2O (polar), CO2 (non-polar - dipoles cancel), NH3 (polar)

Intermolecular Forces

  1. London/dispersion forces: Present in all molecules. Weakest. Increase with electrons/surface area.
  2. Dipole-dipole: Between polar molecules. Permanent dipoles align.
  3. Hydrogen bonding: Special strong dipole-dipole. H bonded to N, O, or F. Explains high BP of H2O, NH3, HF.

Strength: H-bond > dipole-dipole > London forces

Unit 3: States of Matter

Ideal Gas Equation

PV = nRT

P = pressure (Pa), V = volume (m^3), n = moles, R = 8.314 J K^-1 mol^-1, T = temperature (K)

PV = (m/M)RT or PM = dRT (where d = density)

Assumptions of Ideal Gas

  • Particles have negligible volume
  • No intermolecular forces
  • Elastic collisions
  • Average KE proportional to absolute temperature

Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.

Unit 4: Chemical Energetics

Key Definitions

  • Enthalpy change (delta H): Heat change at constant pressure
  • Exothermic: delta H < 0 (heat released, products lower energy)
  • Endothermic: delta H > 0 (heat absorbed, products higher energy)
  • Standard conditions: 298 K, 1 atm, 1 mol dm^-3

Hess's Law

The enthalpy change of a reaction is independent of the pathway taken.

delta H = sum(delta Hf products) - sum(delta Hf reactants)
delta H = sum(delta Hc reactants) - sum(delta Hc products)

Bond Enthalpies

delta H = sum(bonds broken) - sum(bonds formed)

Bond breaking = endothermic (+), Bond forming = exothermic (-)

Enthalpy Definitions

  • Standard enthalpy of formation (delta Hf): Formation of 1 mole from elements in standard states
  • Standard enthalpy of combustion (delta Hc): Complete combustion of 1 mole in oxygen
  • Standard enthalpy of neutralisation: Formation of 1 mole of water from H+ + OH-
  • Lattice energy: Gaseous ions to solid lattice
  • Enthalpy of atomisation: Element in standard state to gaseous atoms

Unit 5: Chemical Equilibria

Equilibrium Constant Kc

aA + bB <=> cC + dD
Kc = [C]^c [D]^d / ([A]^a [B]^b)

Concentrations at equilibrium. Pure solids/liquids omitted.

Kp (for gases)

Kp = (pC)^c (pD)^d / (pA)^a (pB)^b

Partial pressures: pA = mole fraction A x total pressure

Le Chatelier's Principle

If a dynamic equilibrium is disturbed, the system adjusts to minimise the disturbance.

ChangeEffect on equilibrium
Increase concentration of reactantShifts to products
Increase pressure (decrease volume)Shifts to side with fewer moles
Increase temperatureShifts in endothermic direction
Add catalystNo shift (speeds up both directions equally)

Unit 6: Reaction Kinetics

Rate Equations

Rate = k[A]^m [B]^n

m, n = orders with respect to A, B (determined experimentally)

Overall order = m + n

k = rate constant

Determining Order from Graphs

OrderRate vs [A][A] vs timeHalf-life
ZeroHorizontal lineStraight lineDecreases
FirstStraight lineExponential decayConstant
SecondParabolaCurveIncreases

Arrhenius Equation

k = A * e^(-Ea/RT)

A = pre-exponential factor, Ea = activation energy

Taking ln: ln k = ln A - (Ea/R)(1/T)

Plot ln k vs 1/T: gradient = -Ea/R

Activation Energy & Catalysts

  • Activation energy (Ea): Minimum energy for successful collision
  • Catalysts provide alternative pathway with lower Ea
  • Catalysts increase rate but are not consumed
  • Catalysts do NOT change equilibrium position (speed up attainment)

Unit 7: Inorganic Chemistry

Group 2 (Alkaline Earth Metals)

Trends down group:

  • Atomic radius increases (more shells)
  • First IE decreases (more shielding)
  • Reactivity increases (easier to lose e-)
  • Solubility of hydroxides increases
  • Solubility of sulfates decreases

Reactions:

M + 2H2O -> M(OH)2 + H2
MO + H2O -> M(OH)2
MCO3 -> MO + CO2 (on heating)

Group 17 (Halogens)

Trends down group:

  • Atomic radius increases
  • Electronegativity decreases
  • Boiling point increases (more electrons, stronger London forces)
  • Colour darkens (F2 pale yellow, Cl2 green, Br2 red-brown, I2 purple-black)
  • Oxidising power decreases

Displacement: More reactive halogen displaces less reactive from halide.

Cl2 + 2NaBr -> 2NaCl + Br2

Transition Elements (d-block)

Characteristics:

  • Variable oxidation states (e.g., Fe2+/Fe3+, Cu+/Cu2+)
  • Coloured compounds (d-d transitions)
  • Catalytic activity
  • Form complex ions

Ligand substitution: Cu(H2O)6^2+ + 4NH3 -> Cu(NH3)4(H2O)2^2+ + 4H2O

Reactions of Alkenes

  • Hydrogenation: H2, Ni catalyst -> alkane
  • Hydration: H2O, H3PO4 catalyst -> alcohol
  • Addition of HX: Markovnikov's rule - H to C with more H
  • Bromination: Br2/CCl4 -> decolourises (test for unsaturation)
  • Oxidation (cold KMnO4): -> diol
  • Ozonolysis: O3 then Zn/H2O -> carbonyl compounds

Reactions of Alcohols

  • Dehydration (conc H2SO4, 170C): -> alkene
  • Substitution (PCl5, PCl3, SOCl2): -> halogenoalkane
  • Oxidation (K2Cr2O7/H2SO4):
    • Primary alcohol -> aldehyde -> carboxylic acid
    • Secondary alcohol -> ketone
    • Tertiary alcohol -> resistant to oxidation
  • Esterification: Alcohol + carboxylic acid <=> ester + H2O (conc H2SO4)

Test for Carbonyl Compounds

  • 2,4-DNP (Brady's reagent): Orange precipitate with aldehydes and ketones
  • Tollens' reagent: Silver mirror with aldehydes only
  • Fehling's/Benedict's solution: Brick red precipitate with aldehydes only
  • Iodoform test: Yellow precipitate with CH3CO- group (methyl ketones or ethanol)

Unit 10: Analytical Techniques

Mass Spectrometry

  • Molecular ion peak (M+): Highest m/z, gives Mr
  • Base peak: Most abundant ion (tallest)
  • Fragmentation: Breaking of bonds gives characteristic fragments
  • Common fragments: CH3+ (15), C2H5+ (29), C3H7+ (43), CO+ (28)
