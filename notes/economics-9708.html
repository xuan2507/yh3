AS Unit 1: Basic Economic Ideas & Resource Allocation

Fundamental Concepts

  • Scarcity: Limited resources vs unlimited wants
  • Opportunity cost: Next best alternative foregone
  • Production possibility curve (PPC): Shows maximum output combinations
  • Points on PPC: Efficient, full employment
  • Points inside PPC: Inefficient, unemployment
  • Points outside PPC: Unattainable (with current resources)
  • Shift out: Economic growth (more resources/technology)

Positive vs Normative Statements

Fact-based, testableOpinion-based, value judgments
"Inflation is 5%""Inflation is too high"
Can be proven true/falseCannot be proven

Economic Systems

Price mechanism allocatesGovernment allocatesBoth market and government
Private ownershipState ownershipPrivate + public

Division of Labour & Specialisation

Advantages: Increased productivity, lower costs, time-saving, use of machinery

Disadvantages: Boredom, loss of skills, interdependence, structural unemployment

AS Unit 2: The Price System & Theory of the Firm

Demand & Supply

Law of demand: Price up -> quantity demanded down (inverse relationship)

Law of supply: Price up -> quantity supplied up (direct relationship)

Equilibrium: Where demand = supply

Determinants of Demand

  • Income (normal good: demand up; inferior good: demand down)
  • Price of substitutes (price up of A -> demand up for B)
  • Price of complements (price up of A -> demand down for B)
  • Tastes/preferences
  • Population/demographics
  • Expectations

Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

PED = (% change in Qd) / (% change in P)
ValueTypeEffect on revenue
PED > 1ElasticPrice up -> revenue down
PED = 1UnitaryRevenue unchanged
PED < 1InelasticPrice up -> revenue up
PED = 0Perfectly inelasticQuantity fixed
PED = infinityPerfectly elasticAny price change -> zero demand

Determinants of PED: Substitutes, necessity vs luxury, proportion of income, time period, addictive/habitual

Other Elasticities

Income elasticity (YED) = (% change Qd) / (% change income)

Normal good: YED > 0. Inferior good: YED < 0. Luxury: YED > 1.

Cross elasticity (XED) = (% change Qd of A) / (% change P of B)

Substitutes: XED > 0. Complements: XED < 0. Unrelated: XED = 0.

Price elasticity of supply (PES) = (% change Qs) / (% change P)

Consumer & Producer Surplus

  • Consumer surplus: Difference between what consumers are willing to pay and what they actually pay (area below demand, above price)
  • Producer surplus: Difference between price received and minimum price producers would accept (area above supply, below price)

Types of Efficiency

  • Allocative efficiency: P = MC (correct amount produced, welfare maximised)
  • Productive efficiency: Production at minimum AC
  • Dynamic efficiency: Innovation and improvements over time
  • X-inefficiency: Cost higher than minimum due to lack of competitive pressure

Costs of Production

Economies of scale: AC falls as output increases (specialisation, bulk buying, financial, managerial, technical, risk-bearing)

Diseconomies of scale: AC rises as output increases (communication problems, coordination, motivation)

Profit Maximisation

Profit maximised where MC = MR

If MR > MC: produce more. If MR < MC: produce less.

Shut-down point: If P < AVC, firm shuts down in short run.

Break-even: TR = TC (normal profit). Supernormal profit when AR > AC.

AS Unit 3: Government Microeconomic Intervention

Market Failure

When free market fails to allocate resources efficiently.

  • Externalities: Spillover costs/benefits to third parties
  • Public goods: Non-excludable and non-rival (free-rider problem)
  • Merit/demerit goods: Information failure leads to under/over-consumption
  • Monopoly power: Underproduction, higher prices
  • Inequality: Unequal distribution of income/wealth

Government Remedies

ProblemRemedy
Negative externalityTax (Pigouvian), regulations, permits
Positive externalitySubsidies, government provision
Public goodsGovernment provision (tax-funded)
Merit goodsSubsidies, free provision, information campaigns
Demerit goodsTaxes, bans, age restrictions, information
MonopolyRegulation, antitrust, nationalisation

Price Controls

Maximum price (price ceiling): Set below equilibrium. Causes shortage, queueing, black markets.

Minimum price (price floor): Set above equilibrium. Causes surplus, need for government purchase.

Buffer stock: Buy when price low, sell when price high to stabilise.

AS Unit 4: The Macroeconomy & International Trade

Macroeconomic Objectives

  1. Economic growth: Increase in real GDP over time
  2. Low unemployment: Low level of cyclical unemployment
  3. Low inflation: Stable price level (target ~2%)
  4. Balance of payments equilibrium: Sustainable current account
  5. Redistribution of income: Fairer distribution
  6. Protection of environment: Sustainable development

Measuring National Income

GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)

C = consumption, I = investment, G = government spending, X = exports, M = imports

Real GDP: Adjusted for inflation. Nominal GDP at current prices.

GDP per capita: GDP / population

Limitations of GDP: Doesn't measure inequality, environment, informal economy, quality of life

Inflation

Causes:

  • Demand-pull: AD > AS (too much money chasing too few goods)
  • Cost-push: Rising costs (wages, raw materials) -> SRAS shifts left
  • Monetary: Excessive money supply growth
  • Imported: Currency depreciation raises import prices

Effects: Redistributes income, menu/shoe leather costs, uncertainty, reduced international competitiveness

Measurement: CPI (consumer price index) or RPI

Unemployment

TypeCauseCure
FrictionalBetween jobsInformation, job centres
StructuralSkills/location mismatchRetraining, relocation
Cyclical (demand-deficient)Recession, low ADFiscal/monetary stimulus

Balance of Payments

Current account: Trade in goods + trade in services + income + transfers

Capital account: Capital transfers

Financial account: Investment flows

Current account deficit: M > X, spending > income, borrowing from abroad

Remedies for deficit: Expenditure-reducing (contractionary policy), expenditure-switching (devaluation, protectionism)

Comparative Advantage & Trade

Absolute advantage: Can produce more with same resources

Comparative advantage: Lower opportunity cost of production

Countries should specialise in goods where they have comparative advantage and trade.

Protectionism: Tariffs, quotas, embargoes, subsidies, red tape. Protects infant industries, domestic jobs, but reduces efficiency and raises prices.

A2 Unit 5: Microeconomics (Advanced)

Utility Theory

Law of diminishing marginal utility: As consumption increases, MU decreases

Consumer equilibrium: MUa/Pa = MUb/Pb = MUc/Pc

Indifference curves: Combinations giving same utility. Convex to origin (diminishing MRS).

Budget line: Shows maximum combinations affordable. Slope = -Pa/Pb.

Game Theory (Oligopoly)

Prisoner's dilemma: Firms may not cooperate even when it's in their interest.

Nash equilibrium: Each player chooses best strategy given what others do.

Collusion: Formal (cartel) or tacit. Illegal in many countries.

Kinked demand curve: Explains price rigidity in oligopoly. Rivals match price decreases but not increases.

Wage Determination

Perfectly competitive labour market: Wage = MRP (marginal revenue product)

MRP = MPP x MR (marginal physical product x marginal revenue)

Monopsony: Single buyer of labour. Employs where MRP = MCL. Lower wage and employment than competitive.

Trade unions: Can raise wages (collective bargaining) but may cause unemployment.

A2 Unit 6: Macroeconomics (Advanced)

Aggregate Demand & Supply

AD = C + I + G + (X - M)

Shifts in AD: Changes in confidence, interest rates, exchange rates, fiscal policy, world growth

SRAS: Upward sloping (sticky wages/prices). Shifts with input costs, technology, taxes, subsidies.

LRAS: Vertical at full employment output (determined by factors of production). Shifts with productivity, labour force, capital stock.

Fiscal Policy

Government use of taxation and spending to influence AD.

Expansionary: Cut taxes, increase spending -> AD shifts right

Contractionary: Raise taxes, cut spending -> AD shifts left

Automatic stabilisers: Progressive taxes, unemployment benefits (stabilise without government action)

Limitations: Time lags, crowding out, political constraints, Ricardian equivalence

Monetary Policy

Central bank control of interest rates and money supply.

Higher interest rates: Reduce C and I, appreciate currency (reduce X, increase M)

Quantitative easing: Central bank buys bonds to increase money supply

Limitations: Liquidity trap, time lags, effect on exchange rate, asset price bubbles

Exchange Rate Systems

FloatingFixedManaged float
Market determines rateGovernment/peggedMostly market with intervention
Self-correctingStabilityBalance of both
UncertaintyNeed reservesSome flexibility

Appreciation: Currency stronger. Cheaper imports, dearer exports. Reduces inflation.

Depreciation: Currency weaker. Dearer imports, cheaper exports. May cause inflation.

Economic Growth Theories

  • Classical: Growth limited by diminishing returns, Malthusian trap
  • Neo-classical: Savings, capital accumulation, technological progress
  • Endogenous growth: Innovation, human capital, knowledge as driver

Supply-side policies: Education/training, privatisation, deregulation, tax incentives, infrastructure

Essay Writing Guide (25 marks)

Essay Structure

  1. Introduction (3-4 marks): Define key terms, set context, state approach
  2. Analysis paragraphs (12-14 marks):
    • Point - clear topic sentence
    • Explanation - economic theory
    • Diagram - labelled, referenced in text
    • Example - real-world application
    • Link - back to question
  3. Evaluation (6-8 marks):
    • Depends on...
    • However, in reality...
    • Short run vs long run
    • Magnitude/significance
    • Conflicting objectives
    • Stakeholder perspective
  4. Conclusion (2-3 marks):
    • Judgement based on evaluation
    • Prioritise most important factors
    • Consider context/country

Data Response Guide (20 marks)

Approach

  1. Read the question first - know what you're looking for
  2. Analyse the data: trends, patterns, anomalies, calculations
  3. Use the data: Quote figures, calculate % changes
  4. Apply theory: Use relevant economic concepts
  5. Evaluation: Consider limitations of data, alternative explanations

Calculations to Know

% change = [(new - old) / old] x 100
Index number = (current value / base year value) x 100
Real value = (Nominal / Price index) x 100
