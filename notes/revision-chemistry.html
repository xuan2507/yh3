Chemistry 9701 - Last Minute Revision

Condensed essentials for exam day. For full notes, click "Full Notes" above.

Must-Know Formulas & Constants

moles = mass / Mr
moles = concentration x volume (dm^3)
PV = nRT (R = 8.314 J K^-1 mol^-1)
Kc = [products]^coeff / [reactants]^coeff
pH = -log[H+]
pKa = -log(Ka)
rate = k[A]^m[B]^n
k = Ae^(-Ea/RT)
E = mc^2 (mass-energy)

At STP: 1 mole gas = 24 dm^3. At RTP: 1 mole = 24 dm^3.

Avogadro: 6.02 x 10^23 particles per mole

Atomic Structure - Quick Facts

IE trend: Increases across period (more nuclear charge). Decreases down group (more shielding).

Drops: G2 to G3 (p orbital, more shielded). G5 to G6 (pairing repulsion).

Electronic config order: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p...

Exceptions: Cr = [Ar] 3d^5 4s^1, Cu = [Ar] 3d^10 4s^1

Isotopes: Same protons, different neutrons. Same chemical properties, different physical.

Bonding - Quick Reference

BondTypeProperties
IonicMetal + Non-metalHigh MP, soluble, conduct molten/aq
Covalent (simple)Non-metalLow MP, volatile
Covalent (giant)C, SiO2Very high MP, hard
MetallicMetalsConduct, malleable, variable MP

H-bond: H bonded to N, O, or F. Strongest IMF. Explains high BP of H2O, NH3.

VSEPR shapes: 2BP=linear(180), 3BP=trigonal(120), 4BP=tetrahedral(109.5), 3BP+1LP=pyramidal(~107), 2BP+2LP=bent(104.5)

Lone pairs repel MORE than bonding pairs - reduces bond angles

Energetics

Hess's Law: delta H = sum(delta Hf products) - sum(delta Hf reactants)

Bond enthalpy: delta H = bonds broken - bonds formed

Exothermic: delta H < 0 (products lower energy). Endothermic: delta H > 0.

Lattice energy: Higher charge and smaller ions = higher LE

LE proportional to: (q+ x q-) / (r+ + r-)

Equilibria

Kc: Products over reactants, raised to coefficients. Omit pure solids/liquids.

Le Chatelier: System opposes changes

  • Increase concentration reactant -> shifts to products
  • Increase pressure -> shifts to fewer moles
  • Increase temp -> shifts in endothermic direction
  • Catalyst -> no shift, speeds both equally

Only temperature changes K value. Concentration/pressure changes position but K stays same.

Reaction Kinetics

Rate equation: rate = k[A]^m[B]^n

Order from graphs:

  • Zero order: [A] vs t = straight line
  • First order: half-life constant
  • Second order: half-life increases

Arrhenius: ln k = ln A - Ea/RT. Plot ln k vs 1/T, gradient = -Ea/R

Catalyst: Lowers Ea. Equal effect on forward and reverse. Does NOT change K or equilibrium position.

Inorganic Chemistry

Group 2 trend: Down group: more reactive, hydroxides MORE soluble, sulfates LESS soluble

Group 17 trend: Down group: less reactive (oxidising power decreases), BP increases, colour darkens

Halogen displacement: More reactive displaces less reactive from halide solution

Transition metals: Variable oxidation states, coloured compounds (d-d transitions), catalytic activity, complex ions

Ligand substitution: NH3 or Cl- replace H2O ligands. Colour changes.

Organic - Key Reactions

Alkene reactions:

  • H2/Ni -> alkane (hydrogenation)
  • Br2/CCl4 -> dibromoalkane (decolourises - test for C=C)
  • H2O/H+ -> alcohol (hydration)
  • HBr -> bromoalkane (Markovnikov: H to C with more H)
  • Cold KMnO4 -> diol
  • Hot KMnO4 -> cleavage to carbonyls/acids

Alcohol reactions:

  • Primary: oxidises to aldehyde (with distillation) then carboxylic acid
  • Secondary: oxidises to ketone
  • Tertiary: resistant to oxidation
  • Conc H2SO4, 170C -> alkene (elimination)
  • PCl5/PCl3/SOCl2 -> halogenoalkane

Carbonyl tests:

  • 2,4-DNP -> orange ppt (both aldehyde & ketone)
  • Tollens' -> silver mirror (aldehyde only)
  • Fehling's -> brick red ppt (aldehyde only)
  • Iodoform -> yellow ppt (methyl ketone or ethanol)

Analytical Tests

IR peaks:

  • O-H (alcohol): 3230-3550 cm^-1, broad
  • O-H (acid): 2500-3300, very broad
  • C=O: 1640-1750, strong
  • C=C: 1620-1680
  • C-H: 2850-3100

Mass spec: M+ = molecular ion (highest m/z). Base peak = most abundant.

NMR: n+1 rule for splitting. TMS at 0. CHO ~9-10 ppm, COOH ~10-12 ppm.

Quick Tips: Always show curly arrows in mechanisms. Remember reagents AND conditions. For calculations: show working, watch units, use SF correctly. In organic: identify functional groups first.
