Homogeneity of Equations

An equation is homogeneous if the units on both sides are the same. This does NOT prove the equation is correct, but if NOT homogeneous, the equation IS wrong.

Example: Check v^2 = u^2 + 2as is homogeneous.

LHS: [v^2] = (m/s)^2 = m^2 s^-2

RHS: [u^2] = m^2 s^-2, [2as] = (m/s^2)(m) = m^2 s^-2

Both sides = m^2 s^-2. Homogeneous.

Unit 2: Kinematics

SUVAT Equations (Constant Acceleration)

v = u + at
s = ut + (1/2)at^2
s = vt - (1/2)at^2
v^2 = u^2 + 2as
s = (u + v)t / 2

s = displacement, u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time

Displacement-Time Graphs

  • Gradient = velocity
  • Horizontal line = stationary
  • Straight line = constant velocity
  • Curved line = acceleration

Velocity-Time Graphs

  • Gradient = acceleration
  • Area under curve = displacement
  • Horizontal line = constant velocity
  • Straight sloping line = constant acceleration

Acceleration due to Gravity (g)

g = 9.81 m/s^2 (downwards, so use a = -9.81 or a = +9.81 depending on sign convention)

Free fall: u = 0, a = g. Time to fall distance h: h = (1/2)gt^2

Vertical projection: At max height, v = 0. Use v = u - gt to find time.

Projectile Motion

Resolve into horizontal and vertical components:

  • Horizontal: u_x = u cos(theta), a_x = 0, v_x = constant
  • Vertical: u_y = u sin(theta), a_y = -g
  • Time of flight: Use vertical motion, s_y = 0 for landing at same level
  • Range: R = u_x * T (where T = time of flight)
  • Max height: At max height, v_y = 0. Use v_y^2 = u_y^2 - 2gs_y

Unit 3: Dynamics

Newton's Laws of Motion

First Law

A body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a resultant force.

Second Law

F = ma. Resultant force = mass x acceleration.

For varying mass: F = d(mv)/dt = rate of change of momentum

Third Law

If body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts an equal and opposite force on A.

Action and reaction forces act on different bodies.

Linear Momentum

p = mv

Unit: kg m/s or Ns. Vector quantity.

Principle of Conservation of Momentum

For a system with no external resultant force:

Total momentum before = Total momentum after

Collision: m1u1 + m2u2 = m1v1 + m2v2

Explosion: 0 = m1v1 + m2v2 (initially at rest)

Elastic and Inelastic Collisions

TypeMomentumKinetic Energy
ElasticConservedConserved
InelasticConservedNOT conserved
Perfectly inelasticConservedMaximum loss (bodies stick)

For elastic collision: relative speed of approach = relative speed of separation

Impulse

Impulse = F * t = change in momentum = mv - mu

Impulse = area under force-time graph

Unit 4: Forces, Density & Pressure

Types of Forces

  • Gravitational (Weight): W = mg (acts downwards)
  • Normal contact force: Perpendicular to surface
  • Tension: In strings/rods (pulls)
  • Friction: Opposes motion/attempted motion
  • Drag/Air resistance: Opposes motion through fluid
  • Upthrust: Upwards force in fluid (Archimedes)
  • Electrostatic: Between charges

Moments

Moment = F * d (force x perpendicular distance from pivot)

Unit: Nm. Principle of moments: sum of clockwise moments = sum of anticlockwise moments

Centre of Gravity

The point where the entire weight of an object appears to act. For uniform objects, it's at the geometric centre.

Density & Pressure

Density: rho = m/V (kg/m^3)
Pressure: P = F/A (Pa or N/m^2)
Fluid pressure: P = rho * g * h

For a liquid column: pressure increases with depth

Unit 5: Work, Energy & Power

Key Equations

Work: W = F * s * cos(theta)
KE = (1/2)mv^2
GPE = mgh
Power: P = W/t = Fv
Efficiency = (useful output / total input) x 100%

Principle of Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred from one form to another.

Total energy in an isolated system is constant.

Work Done by a Gas

W = p * delta(V)

Work done BY gas = area under p-V graph

Unit 6: Deformation of Solids

Hooke's Law

F = kx (up to limit of proportionality)

k = spring constant (N/m), x = extension (m)

Stress, Strain & Young Modulus

Stress = F/A (Pa)
Strain = extension / original length (no units)
Young Modulus E = stress/strain = (F/A)/(e/L) = FL/Ae

Stress-Strain Graph Features

  • Limit of proportionality: End of straight-line region (Hooke's law)
  • Elastic limit: Beyond this, permanent deformation occurs
  • Yield point: Large strain for small increase in stress
  • Ultimate tensile stress (UTS): Maximum stress before breaking
  • Breaking stress: Stress at fracture

Unit 7: Waves

Wave Equations

v = f * lambda
f = 1/T
Intensity = Power / Area

Progressive Waves

Transfer energy without transferring matter.

  • Amplitude (A): Maximum displacement from equilibrium
  • Wavelength (lambda): Distance between identical points
  • Frequency (f): Number of oscillations per second
  • Period (T): Time for one complete oscillation
  • Phase difference: phi = 2pi * (x/lambda) or phi = 2pi * (t/T)

Unit 8: Superposition

Principle of Superposition

When two or more waves meet, the resultant displacement is the vector sum of the individual displacements.

Stationary (Standing) Waves

  • Formed by superposition of two progressive waves of same frequency traveling in opposite directions
  • Nodes: Points of zero amplitude (destructive interference)
  • Antinodes: Points of maximum amplitude (constructive interference)
  • Distance between adjacent nodes = lambda/2
  • Distance between node and antinode = lambda/4

String fixed at both ends: L = n(lambda/2) where n = 1, 2, 3...

Pipe open at both ends: L = n(lambda/2)

Pipe closed at one end: L = n(lambda/4) where n = 1, 3, 5...

Diffraction

Spreading of waves when passing through a gap or around an obstacle.

  • Maximum diffraction when gap width ~ wavelength
  • Diffraction is most significant when obstacle/gap is comparable to wavelength

Double-Slit Interference

Fringe spacing: w = lambda * D / a

w = fringe spacing, lambda = wavelength, D = slit-screen distance, a = slit separation

  • Bright fringes: path difference = n*lambda
  • Dark fringes: path difference = (n + 1/2)*lambda

Unit 9: Electricity

Key Equations

Q = It (Charge = current x time)
V = W/Q (Potential difference = work/charge)
V = IR (Ohm's Law)
P = VI = I^2R = V^2/R
R = rho * L / A

Ohm's Law & Resistance

For an ohmic conductor at constant temperature: V proportional to I.

Resistivity (rho): Material property. Unit: ohm-metre.

Unit 10: D.C. Circuits

Kirchhoff's Laws

First Law (Junction/Current Law)

Sum of currents entering a junction = Sum of currents leaving

I1 + I2 = I3 + I4

Second Law (Loop/Voltage Law)

Sum of EMFs around any closed loop = Sum of p.d.s around the loop

Resistor Combinations

Series: R_total = R1 + R2 + R3
Parallel: 1/R_total = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3

For two in parallel: R_total = (R1 * R2)/(R1 + R2)

Potential Divider

V_out = V_in * (R2 / (R1 + R2))

Used with thermistors and LDRs in sensor circuits.

EMF and Internal Resistance

E = I(R + r) = V + Ir

E = EMF, r = internal resistance, V = terminal p.d.

Terminal p.d. = EMF - lost volts (Ir)

Short circuit current: I = E/r
