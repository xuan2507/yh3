Speaking Practice

Part 1: Introduction & Interview (4-5 min)

Answer questions about yourself, your home, work, studies, interests.

Hometown Work/Study Hobbies Daily Routine Food Transport

Q: Do you work or are you a student?

A model: I'm currently a student preparing for my A-Level examinations. I study at a private college in Kuala Lumpur, where I'm taking Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics. It's quite demanding, but I find the subjects fascinating, especially when we do practical experiments in the lab.

Part 2: Long Turn (3-4 min)

Speak for 1-2 minutes on a given topic with 1 minute preparation time.

Describe a skill you would like to learn in the future.
  • What the skill is
  • Why you want to learn it
  • How you would learn it
  • And explain how this skill would be useful to you

Part 3: Discussion (4-5 min)

Abstract questions related to the Part 2 topic — test opinions, comparisons, predictions.

Q: Why do you think some people find it difficult to learn new skills later in life?

Framework: Acknowledge → Reason 1 (time/commitments) → Reason 2 (fear of failure) → Contrast (others thrive) → Conclusion

Writing Practice

Task 1: Report (Academic) / Letter (General)

Academic

Question: The line graph shows the percentage of people aged 65 and over in three countries from 1940 to 2040. Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features.

Band 7+ Checklist

  • Introduction: paraphrase the question (no numbers)
  • Overview: 2 main trends (highest/lowest, increases/decreases)
  • Body 1: detailed comparison with data
  • Body 2: remaining features with data
  • No opinion, no conclusion, no bullet points

Task 2: Essay (250 words, 40 min)

Question: In many countries, traditional foods are being replaced by international fast food. This is having a negative effect on families and communities. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Essay Plan (Agree/Disagree)

Intro: Paraphrase + thesis (strongly agree)
Body 1: Family impact — less home cooking → weaker family bonds, shared meals decline
Body 2: Community impact — local restaurants close, homogenization of high streets, loss of cultural identity
Body 3: Counter-argument + refutation — convenience valued, but health costs and social erosion outweigh
Conclusion: Restate position + summary of main points

Reading Strategies

Skimming & Scanning

Skimming: Read first & last sentence of each paragraph to grasp main idea (60 sec per passage).

Scanning: Use keywords from question to locate specific information quickly.

Question Types

  • TFNG True / False / Not Given — exact match vs inference vs not mentioned
  • Matching Headings / Features / Sentence endings — find synonyms
  • Gap-fill Summary / Note / Table / Diagram — word limit crucial
  • Multiple Choice Single or multiple — eliminate distractors

Time Management

Passage 1 (easiest): 15 min

Passage 2: 20 min

Passage 3 (hardest): 25 min

Never spend more than 90 seconds on a single question. Guess and move on.

Common Traps

  • NG vs False: If the text doesn't mention it → NOT GIVEN. If it contradicts → FALSE.
  • Word limit: "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" — hyphenated words count as one.
  • Synonyms: The answer rarely uses the exact words from the question.

Listening Strategies

Section 1: Social/Transactional (2 speakers, everyday context)

Usually a phone conversation — booking, enquiry, application. Expect names, numbers, dates, prices.

Tip: Write numbers as digits. Dates can be said in multiple formats — write what you hear.

Section 2: Informational (1 speaker, social context)

A monologue — tour guide, introduction, announcement. Often includes a map or diagram labeling.

Tip: Look at the map/diagram before audio starts. Identify key landmarks and directions.

Section 3: Educational (2-4 speakers, academic discussion)

Students and tutor discussing a project, assignment, or course. Multiple-choice and matching common.

Tip: Note who agrees/disagrees. Speakers may change their minds — the final opinion is what matters.

Section 4: Academic Lecture (1 speaker)

A university lecture on an academic topic. Gap-fill (summary/note/table) most common.

Tip: Predict word type (noun/verb/adjective) from grammar. Listen for signpost words: "firstly", "however", "in conclusion".
